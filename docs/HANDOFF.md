## Start here (2026-10-06)

**What you can play today.** Make a trainer (Rei by default) and wake up in Bramblewick. Professor Hazel gives you a starter, a Growlithe, 5 Poke Balls and (on your next visit) 3 Treats, then your rival Sunniva battles you. Outside the north gate, herds of 45 Kanto species roam Hearthmeadow, and both friends in a world meet the same herds. You can:
- sneak up and throw balls in the overworld (hold Q, or right mouse);
- fight wild 2v2 battles, in tactical or action mode;
- catch creatures in battle;
- survive a furious creature after a failed catch by dodge-rolling (V), healing (H), throwing a Treat or sending your partner to cut it off (F);
- climb cliffs, big rocks and steep slopes anywhere by walking into them, as far as your stamina lasts;
- find 17 supply caches (the best ones on top of rocks and mesas), 10 Lysfolk tablets and 9 pages of Hazel's lost field notes around Hearthmeadow, and trade tablet rubbings to Hazel for Great Balls;
- rest at either house door;
- manage a party of 6, a PC box and the region Dex;
- gather wood, stone, fibre, Oran Berries, Red Apricorns, copper ore and wild mushrooms (E), make a stone hatchet and pick by hand, cook Treats and mushroom skewers and boil water at the campfire, and craft your own Poke Balls, Potions and canteens at the Bramblewick workbench once you reach trainer level 3 (K opens crafting);
- keep yourself fed and watered: hunger and thirst run down out in the wild, so eat from the bag, drink at any river, lake or pond (E), and carry river water in a canteen;
- earn prize money from battles and spend it at Bramblewick's Field Supplies, Apothecary, Wayfarer Inn and The Clothier, or sell what you gather there for a quarter of its value;
- spend Technology Points (one per trainer level and per tablet read) to learn a stone sickle and cloth armour, then wear the armour to soften charging Pokémon;
- let your lead Pokémon lend a hand: a Rock or Ground type digs up more stone, a Grass type more wood and fibre, a Water type finds clean drinking water and a Fire type cooks faster;
- take on Hearthmeadow's Alpha, a huge red-eyed Lv. 18 Ponyta that guards a gully between the western mesas and charges anyone who comes near. Beat it for a big prize and a Great Ball, or catch it, once a day;
- battle eight roaming trainers along the roads, each with a themed doubles team. They spot you and walk over to challenge you, Pokémon style, unless you sneak past behind them, and pay prize money the first time and a smaller rematch prize on later days;
- find the hay bale that rustles in the meadow west of Route 1, just south of the junction. Sten, a Krane Industries runaway, lives in it and trades what he knows for Mushroom Skewers: a bit of what Team Tether is up to and the way to a supply cache, marked on your map;
- climb the hill to the lone tree, where Gudrun, the Competitive Picnicker, has a picnic and a grill. Bring three Wild Mushrooms and beat her in a timed cook-off (chop, stir, season) to learn her Hearty Stew, the most filling food in the game. Her grill is a campfire anyone can cook at;
- take side quests from people with a gold "!" over their heads: Pelle by Bramblewick's well wants to see a Pikachu, Farmer Arne wants six wild Rattata knocked out, Innkeeper Bodil needs mushrooms and berries for the inn's pot, and Kari at the pond wants to see a Poliwag and a Psyduck. A "?" means you can hand one in;
- meet Edvin, the Overconfident Cartographer, at his easel on a rise west of Route 1 north of the junction. His maps are wrong: go and look at the Old Arch, the standing stones and the lake jetty, tell him what's really there, and he gives you a real treasure map. The chest is far to the west and takes a Stone Pick to dig up.
- watch the day turn to night. A day lasts 30 real minutes (20 of daylight, 10 of night) on a clock both friends share. Nights are blue and moonlit, with stars, and Bramblewick's lanterns and the campfire light up their surroundings;
- meet different Pokémon at night: the birds and bugs mostly sleep, while Oddish, Meowth, Clefairy and Jigglypuff come out and Zubat flock over the meadows;
- light a lantern after dark (L): make one at the workbench or buy one, and it burns about two nights of oil. Each night a dozen bits of stardust fall in the meadows under columns of starlight, the same for both friends, and you can only find them in the grass by lantern light (yours or your friend's). The shops pay well for them.

In co-op, a friend can join your battle and throw their own balls. Everything ships to the live site on merge.

## Canon (keep consistent)

- Region **Sijord** (fjords and meadows). Hometown **Bramblewick**, a small fenced village south-centre in **Hearthmeadow Vale**, with both players' houses and **Professor Hazel**'s lab. Route 1 leaves through the north gate.
- Villains **Team Tether**, hidden inside **Krane Industries** (HQ in Jernhamn). Rival **Sunniva**.
- Classes (code ids): `ranger`, `tamer`, `artisan`, `scholar`, `medic`. Each has one small perk and one real cost (DESIGN §7.2, `src/shared/classes.ts`).
- 12 large cities, 10 of them with gyms. **Crownspire** (Elite Four, 10 badges) and **Tornhavn** (Battle Tower, 5 badges) are the only hard-locked places.
- World axes: **north = +Z, east = -X**.
- Environment art is procedural. As of 2026-10-05, the user explicitly requested imported Pokémon character models and Red from Pokémon Masters, publicly hosted. See [MODEL_ASSETS.md](MODEL_ASSETS.md).

## Stack and layout

TypeScript + Three.js, built with Vite, tested with Vitest. The environment is procedural; imported character/item assets are self-hosted with procedural fallbacks.

```
src/client/main.ts    boot: title -> trainer creator -> loading screen -> Game
src/client/core       game loop (game.ts), renderer and post (render.ts), input, collision, save, settings + keybinds
src/client/world      terrain, sky, water, grass, vegetation, cliffs, fauna, POIs, Bramblewick (hometown.ts), layout
src/client/player     trainer model and rig, animation clips and IK math, movement controller, camera, remote partner
src/client/npc        Professor Hazel, Sunniva (rival), the roaming trainers, Sten in his hay bale, Gudrun's picnic, the side quest givers
src/client/battle     in-world battles: stage (ring, effects), director (playback, camera, prompts), battle UI
src/client/overworld  wild herds, the follower creature, creature movement
src/client/creatures  procedural creature models and animation
src/client/ui         title, creator, HUD (compass, minimap, quests), dialogue, menus (map, bag, party, quests, settings)
src/client/net        co-op: p2p.ts (default, Trystero) and client.ts (optional Node server)
src/server            optional Node WebSocket server (2 players per room)
src/shared            types, protocol, classes, items; battle/ (engine, stats, damage, AI); data/ (species, moves, abilities)
tests/                Vitest unit tests (battle engine and damage calc, data, controller, camera, animation math, collision, settings, rooms)
```

In dev builds the running game is exposed as `window.sijord` for debugging, with `debugGiveParty`, `debugWildBattle`, `debugTeleport` and `debugTimeScale` (speeds game time up, handy in the container's slow software renderer).

## Run, test, build, deploy

```bash
npm install
npm run dev            # http://localhost:5173
npm run typecheck
npm test               # 253 tests as of 2026-10-06
npm run build          # dist/ (what GitHub Pages serves)
npm run build:single   # dist-single/sijord.html, game code in a double-clickable file; imported art still needs internet
```

- **Live site:** https://shamygo.github.io/Sijord/. `.github/workflows/pages.yml` runs tests, builds and deploys on every push to `main`. Pages source is set to "GitHub Actions". The repo is public, which is what makes free Pages hosting possible.
- **Downloadable build:** `.github/workflows/release.yml` rebuilds `sijord.html` on every push to `main` and attaches it to the `latest` release ("Latest playable build").
- **Co-op:** both players type the same world code on the title screen. Default transport is peer-to-peer WebRTC via Trystero, using free public Nostr relays only for matchmaking, so nothing is hosted. Fallbacks: `npm run relay` plus `?relay=ws://<ip>:8090`, or `npm run server` plus `?server=ws://<ip>:8787` (or `?server=local`). Co-op over the public relays has not yet been confirmed on Simon's and his friend's real machines.
- **Verifying visually:** Chromium and Playwright are typically available in Claude's cloud container. Run `npm run dev` or `npm run preview` and screenshot the game. Software rendering there is very slow, so judge frame rate on real hardware, not in the container. [DEV_NOTES.md](DEV_NOTES.md#how-to-play-test-in-a-claude-cloud-container) has the exact Playwright setup.

**M3 overworld throws (2026-10-06):** hold **Q** (or the right mouse button) to aim: the camera pulls in over the shoulder, Rei holds the wound-up throw, a dotted arc and a landing ring show exactly where the ball will go (the preview runs the same fixed physics steps as the real ball, `src/client/overworld/ball-flight.ts`), and the arc turns gold with a lock ring and a name, level and HP panel when it will hit a creature. The wheel picks the ball, E cancels, releasing (or a left click) throws. Balls fly, bounce off the ground and walls, roll and stop; a ball only catches while still in its first arc. A hit plays the catch in the world (the creature shrinks into the ball, it drops and shakes, then a sparkle or a burst, `src/client/overworld/throw.ts`). The overworld throw is worth ×0.6, but ×1.5 when the creature hasn't noticed you (grazing or wandering, not alert, and you're outside its 105° view either side), so sneaking up from behind matters. Missed balls lie in the grass with a twinkle for 150 s and E picks them up; balls landing in deep water are lost; a landing ball alerts creatures within 4.5 m. When a creature breaks free it reacts by temperament (DESIGN §5.3, `src/shared/overworld-catch.ts`): skittish ones bolt with their skittish herd-mates, docile ones back off warily, either may turn and fight on an aggro roll that rises when it outlevels you, territorial ones start a battle, and aggressive ones charge you directly: they close in, stop dead for a 0.7 s wind-up, then lunge in a straight line you can sidestep or dodge (**V**: a roll along your input, or a backstep hop with none; hits pass through for its first 0.3 s and it costs stamina), for up to 20 s or until you leave their territory by 40 m (E battles a charging creature). A lunge that connects knocks the trainer down and costs HP (`src/shared/trainer-vitals.ts`: 100 HP plus 2 per trainer level scaled by class, damage by the creature's level, a short guard after each hit). HP only comes back after 12 s with nothing attacking, at 0.5 HP/s, or 30 at once with **H** (uses a Potion). At 0 HP you black out to Hazel's lab like a team wipe. The HP bar appears only while hurt. Your friend sees your throws, catches and knockdowns replayed (snapshots carry `ballThrow`, `ballCatch` and `down`). V is used rather than DESIGN §17's Ctrl/Alt because Ctrl+W closes the browser tab and Alt+D jumps to the address bar. Screenshots: [aiming](screenshots/2026-10-06-catching/overworld-aim.png) (compare `docs/reference/2026-10-05/ui-catching.png`), [caught](screenshots/2026-10-06-catching/overworld-caught.png), [knocked down](screenshots/2026-10-06-catching/charge-knockdown.png), [wild Pikachu](screenshots/2026-10-06-catching/wild-pikachu.png). Gaps: the knockdown tips the whole rig over stiffly rather than playing a fall clip; there is no crouch/sneak key (only facing counts), and herd positions drift apart between the two screens, so a friend's ball can land beside the creature on your screen.

**Treats and partner interceptions (2026-10-06, DESIGN §5.3):** a **Treat** (`treat`, a honey biscuit) is thrown like a ball: while aiming, the wheel cycles your balls and then Treats. A Treat that hits a creature, or lands within 7 m of one, draws the best eater: the creature it hit, else a furious one (charging you), else the nearest. A furious creature calms down to eat it but stays wary, so it isn't unaware. A calm one eating a Treat counts as unaware from any side (×1.5 catch odds) unless it was already alert. Eating takes 8 s, after which the creature won't turn on you for 30 s. An untouched Treat lies there for 25 s and draws in anything that wanders close. Hazel gives 3 Treats on your next visit after the starter, and 2 more only when you've run out. The code is `TREAT`, `treat()` and the `eat` state in `src/client/overworld/wild.ts`, the Treat flight in `src/client/overworld/throw.ts`, and `makeTreat` in `src/client/battle/fx.ts`. **Interception:** while a creature charges you within 14 m, the prompt offers **F** to send your lead Pokémon. It rushes the attacker (`Follower.rushAt`), and when it gets there a battle starts against that creature alone, without its herd-mate (`updateIntercept` in `src/client/core/game.ts`). F still calls or recalls the partner the rest of the time. Tests: `tests/treats.test.ts`. Gaps: Treats can't be used in battle yet (DESIGN's obedience bonus), and the friend sees the Treat fly but not the creature eating it, because behaviour runs per player.

**Movement, dodge roll and climbing (2026-10-06).** Jordan said the running looked choppy and asked for a rolling dodge and Palworld/BotW climbing that doesn't need a ladder.
- *Gait* (`src/client/player/imported-trainer.ts`): walk, jog and run play together on one shared stride phase. When Rei loads, each gait's stride length (how far a planted foot sweeps back per cycle) and phase offset (left foot furthest forward) are measured from the clips. Each frame the phase advances by speed ÷ the blended stride, and every gait is posed at its own offset. Jog blends in over 1.4-2.6 m/s and run over 5.4-7.0 m/s. All clip weights ease (τ 0.07 s) and are normalised, so nothing pops. Walk and run were re-baked so a planted foot sweeps back at a constant speed (the library sprint sped up and slowed mid-contact, which read as skating). Measured in the browser, a planted foot slides 0.1 m/s jogging and 0.2 m/s sprinting. ChatGPT's hip correction (POLISH_HANDOFF) is gone.
- *Dodge roll:* **V** with a direction held rolls about 3.4 m that way in 0.5 s, turning into it; with no direction it is still the backstep hop. You can't jump out of a roll.
- *Climbing* (`src/client/player/climbing.ts`; `tryGrab`, `stepWall` and `stepMantle` in `controller.ts`):
  - Walk head-on into climbable rock and Rei grabs on after a short beat. A ledge up to 1.3 m is vaulted instead, and one a little higher is pulled up onto.
  - On the wall, W A S D move (1.25 m/s up). Space leaps 1.4 m (sideways with A or D), S+Space kicks off backwards, and C lets go. At the top Rei pulls up automatically when there's room to stand; past a step too narrow to stand on, Rei reaches across to the wall behind it.
  - Stamina drains 0.075/s while moving and 0.02/s while hanging, and a leap costs 0.18. At zero Rei falls and can't climb or sprint until it recovers. One bar is about 17 m of wall: the first mesa's south face from (265, 100) took it to 0.03. That's deliberate (hard game).
  - Ladders grab the same way (walk into one, or press C); W and S climb and Space lets go.
- *What is climbable:* terrain steeper than 45°, and colliders marked `climb: true`. Mesa cliff columns are now oriented boxes (`kind: 'obox'`, `src/client/world/cliffs.ts`) with `maxY` at their tops, and big rocks (`world/vegetation.ts`) carry `maxY`, `climb` and a `dome`. Trees, houses and fences only block. A collider with `maxY` stops blocking once your feet are above it, and climbable tops can be stood on (`surfaceHeightAt` in `world/index.ts`).

**Day and night (2026-10-07, ROADMAP M5).**
- *The clock* (`src/shared/daynight.ts`): the time of day comes from the wall clock (`clockAt(Date.now())`), not from when the page loaded, so both friends see the same sky without sending anything. One day is 30 real minutes: sunrise at 06:00, sunset at 19:00, 20 real minutes of daylight (about 0.65 game minutes a second) and 10 of night (1.1 a second). `isNight` (19:50 to 05:35) is the window where a lamp or lantern matters. Nothing is saved: the time is never stored. The HUD clock (top right, ☀ or ☾) shows it.
- *The look* (`src/client/world/daynight.ts`): keyframed looks by game hour (night, blue hour, dawn, day, golden hour, sunset, dusk) blend into each other. Each sets the sky gradient, fog colour, hemisphere light, key light colour and strength, exposure, stars and a tint for clouds and water. Daytime is exactly the old look. The sun rises in the east (-X), crosses the southern sky and sets in the west; once it is well down the key light swaps to the moon, high in the south-west, both faint around the swap so nothing jumps. Nights are blue and readable, the Arceus way, not black: exposure goes up to 1.55 and the moon still casts soft shadows.
- *Shared uniforms* (`SKY_UNIFORMS` in `sky.ts`): the sky dome, clouds and water read the same sky colours, stars, disc and light tint, so one call (`applyTimeOfDay`, every frame from `world.setTimeOfDay`) changes them all. `SKY.sunDir` and `SKY.sunColor` are shared by reference and point at the moon at night. The game copies the fog colour to the scene and sets the pipeline's exposure (`RenderPipeline.setExposure`).
- *Lamplight* (`src/client/world/lamplight.ts`): every lantern (`lantern()` and the cottage door lamps add their spot to `kit.lamps`) and the campfire are light sources. Three point lights follow the lamps nearest the player after dark, fading out before they move to another lamp so nothing pops; the campfire's light is brighter and flickers. They always exist (dark by day), because adding or removing lights rebuilds every shader. The glowing lamp boxes also brighten at night.
- *Testing:* `window.sijord.debugTimeOfDay(21.5)` jumps to 21:30 and holds the clock there for screenshots; `debugTimeOfDay(21.5, false)` lets it run on from there and `debugTimeOfDay(null)` goes back to the shared clock. `render_game_to_text()` has `time`. Tests: `tests/daynight.test.ts` (the clock, the day and night looks, no jumps minute to minute, and the lamplight handover).
- *Browser check (2026-10-07):* Route 1 at dawn, noon, golden hour, sunset, dusk and midnight (`day-cycle.jpg`: 06:30, 12:30, 18:00, 19:18, 20:06, 23:00), Bramblewick's plaza and main street at night (lamplight on the walls, the flowers and the player) and the campsite at night, on Medium quality. Screenshots: [docs/screenshots/2026-10-07-daynight](screenshots/2026-10-07-daynight).
- *Night herds:* each spawn-table entry has an optional `night` weight (`ZONES` in `src/client/overworld/wild.ts`). At night Pidgey, Spearow, Caterpie and Weedle mostly sleep, Oddish, Meowth, Clefairy and Jigglypuff are far more common, and Zubat (weight 0 by day near town) flock over the meadows. Night is folded into the herd window as its low bit (`epoch * 2 + night`), so herds rolled after dusk are a fresh roll under their own keys, the same for both friends; herds already out stay until they despawn, and a friend still in the day herds is met in them. Tests: `tests/wild-shared.test.ts`. Browser check (2026-10-07): the same five-stop tour round Route 1 met Pidgey, Caterpie, Weedle and Ponyta at 13:00 (35 creatures) and Zubat, Meowth, Clefairy and Jigglypuff at 22:30 (21), with no Pidgey, Caterpie or Weedle.
- *Gaps:* no sleeping through the night, and no night-only species beyond the reweighting (no Gastly or Hoothoot yet). Co-op relies on both computers' clocks being right: a clock a real minute off puts that friend's sky 40-65 game minutes apart. The warm sun haze in the distance fog is still baked at the noon sun's direction (the fog chunk in `sky.ts`), though it fades out at night. The moon has no phases, and lamps only bloom on Medium and High.
