# Sijord: Simon's vision, in his own words

This file preserves the original requests from Simon (GitHub `Shamygo`), the project owner, as he wrote them. They are the source of truth for what the game should be. [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md) turns them into concrete systems; where the two disagree, this file and Simon's latest word win.

The art reference he attached is [reference/art-reference.png](reference/art-reference.png) (described in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md#the-art-reference)).

## 1. The original spec (2026-10-04)

> I want you to create a browser game that is portable to Windows and Mac that is a Pokemon game in the style of the video game Power World. It should have a large map uh, filled with, you know, Pokemon in different biomes, in different areas. I want, in the beginning, you to spawn into a conventional town the way you do in regular Pokemon games. I want there to be three different types of cities you encounter throughout the map for this game. Small, medium, and large. Small being something more akin to cities or towns from Legends Arceus, where they're small, kind of fenced in, more stereotypical villages, less substance, obviously. Medium towns, right? The second most common type after the small type. The medium villages containing more substance, but there's not necessarily, say, skyscrapers or, you know, tons of NPCs or tons of things, enough substance to fit what would be classified as a medium town. And then finally, large cities, of which there will only be about 10 to 12. These are much larger. These would contain the gym leaders for the region, and um, only one or two of these cities would be locked behind a progressional wall, obviously one being the Elite Four, and one being maybe the Battle Tower, for this region.
>
> Now, the idea is that this game, first and foremost, is open world, meaning you can, for the most part, go anywhere. You could, at the very beginning, cross the entirety of the map to go to the other side, just like how you can in PAL World. The difference here is that the game will adjust itself in certain areas based on what you do in what point of the map. Say you defeat one of the gym leaders on the other side of the map, instead of the one closest to where you originally enter the game. Well, depending on what level they were when you beat them, the other gym leaders will adapt to that level system and now put themselves at the next level cap. There should also be level caps for Pokemon and gym leaders, meaning there's a certain level you can get to before you can't go any higher until you challenge the next gym leader. This is not to say that you can't catch Pokemon that are higher level, but it does speak to the fact that if you do, they'll be harder to catch, they may not listen to you, and they won't be usable in the gym battle until you get to the level requirement where they are able to or beat the certain checkpoints necessary.
>
> Furthermore, like Power World, this should be a survival craft-esque game where there is the capability to build and gather resources and you know set up structures as long as they're not within the bounds of any city. So anywhere outside of the cities is usually fair game.
>
> In a similar fashion to Pokemon, you should look back on all the previous Pokemon games and see the patterns they use for deciding which Pokemon will be in the game. See how they decide. Usually, the games, the pattern they'll decide is they'll do around 400 returning Pokemon from several previous generations, as well as 100 new Pokemon. I want you to do something similar. Look at the pattern that they use to decide that, mimic that pattern, right, in a new way, and create a hundred or so, whatever, new Pokemon in addition to that.
>
> Moreover, there should also be levels for both people and for Pokemon. Obviously, Pokemon more conventionally speaking, but for people, there should also be level ups as well. When you spawn into the world, there should be four or five classes each player can choose from. And depending on those classes, you'll have some benefits that other classes don't have, and other classes will have some benefits that your class doesn't have. Additionally, you'll level up over time, which will allow you to spend skill points you get from leveling up into your skill tree, making it easier to traverse and to move and to catch Pokemon and things of that nature that should be specialized to whatever class you picked.
>
> Moreover, this is to be a multiplayer game. And the way that functions is as such. First and foremost, every battle in the game is a double battle, right? But the only double battles in the entire game that should utilize the multiplayer function are, in parentheses, boss battles. So that is to say Elite Four, gym leaders, things of that nature. And the only time you would be able to do those multiplayer battles is if you and the other player in the world were in the building together at the same time to challenge those boss battles. If you are not, the boss battle won't trigger because both players are not present. Other than that, every other battle is each player's own individual battle, and those are not collaborative efforts only the boss battles. Other than that, every single battle in the game is a double battle. If it's an individual player battle, you as a player will send out two Pokemon, the ones that are the two in the front of your party. If it's a multiplayer battle, meaning with the other person in the world, you will both bring three Pokemon of your choice to the battle, and you'll both throw out one, and you'll be able to work together to fight the bosses.
>
> With that being said, the battles in this game should be difficult. They should be competitive level battles that are very difficult to get over. Bosses should have items, they should have good moves, they should think intelligently. And to that same end, it should not be impossible to get battle items on our end as well. You should have the capability to find certain battle items and to make the strongest teams possible to give yourself an edge against these bosses.
>
> Additionally, similar to Pal World, I want a type of aspect to this game that utilizes the like necessity for armor, but not necessarily for weapons. Tools and armor, yes. Weapons, not necessarily. And what I mean by that is, is that there should also, first of all, be a hunger and water system for the game. But there should also be a system where if you're going to catch Pokemon, if you fail the like one of your catches or something like that, the Pokemon can be aggressive in response to your person. So that's why, depending on how high level a Pokemon might be that you run into, it may be smarter to weaken them first in battle before you try to catch them. Because if you just try to catch them outright, the closer they are to your level, the easier it would be. But if they're too high a level or a few levels higher than you, it will be more difficult and they may try to attack you. So having certain armor could make that easier and have a more simpler time surviving that and ultimately maybe engaging in a battle and catching them.
>
> Moreover, with the survival craft aspect, there should be the ability for automation eventually. For example, Pokeballs, eventually you may be able to automate a system for crafting those. Or money, there may be a way to automate making money because prices in cities might be a little bit higher just so things don't come to you very easily. Again, the game should be difficult. Doable, but difficult.
>
> Additionally, some Pokemon should have a riding feature. So in other words, some Pokemon would have, depending on the type of Pokemon, obviously, and what they look like, some maybe can be ridden on their backs, like on the ground. Some can be ridden like in the water. Some can be ridden as like a flying Pokemon. Now, which Pokemon can do which depends obviously on the design for the Pokemon. Some may have none of those capabilities. Some may have one or multiple of those capabilities. And how efficiently they do those capabilities depends Pokemon to Pokemon. So one Pokemon that can be ridden on its back on land, say like a Bouffalant, if he was in the game, would be much slower than a riding mount like a Cyclozar, which would be much faster, right? So it would depend on the design of the Pokemon and maybe their stats would kind of lead you to understand how fast or slow they are.
>
> Additionally, I would like um, certain mechanics from prior Pokemon games to be in this game. Think Mega Evolution, think Z moves, um, Gigantamax, things of that nature. I would also like, in addition to all these background features, a story to the game. It doesn't have to be the greatest story in the world, but I want it to have substance and I want it to carry the player throughout the game as you go check mark to check mark, right? In similar Pokemon game fashion.
>
> Moreover, I want a questing system throughout the game, a questing system that can give you XP for person level up, player level up, as well as um, send you on adventures and maybe help you capture mythical Pokemon or encounter legendaries. Maybe that is a way to do so, is through questing objectives. I also don't want anything to be locked behind the post game. Anything that you can do in the game should be achievable, even if some are more difficult than others, prior to the end game, prior even to the Elite Four.
>
> Moreover, every um, Pokemon gym where you're gonna challenge a gym leader should be in totality a multiplayer effort. Meaning to even start the gym, both players have to be present. And once they are, and it's confirmed that, you will then have to accomplish a multiplayer challenge. Think like the game Portal almost, where it's two players collaboratively solving a gym challenge where both are necessary to do it. And then eventually they fight a handful of trainers along the way and then the gym leader together.
>
> Again, both artistically and from a very basic structural standpoint, it should look and play in a similar fashion to Power World. Additionally, similar to other Pokemon games, there should be, first and foremost, a customize your own player screen. So you can, it doesn't need to be anything necessarily overly complex, but the ability to differentiate characters so not every playable character looks the same. Additionally, the game should start very much in Pokemon game fashion. You start out, you're in your hometown, um, both players would have their own house in that hometown, just as a starting point. We would have to go talk to the professor, and then once you leave the starting town, the open world experience begins in full.
>
> One important thing to remember is as you play through the game, it should feel substantive. It should not feel empty. One big gripe with Power World is there's a lot of empty space, even though it feels like there's a lot to do. I want there not only to be a lot to do and have a lot of space, but the space have substance. Not just random trainers, but events or things to do or you know quirky characters. Fun things to add an additional layer of depth to the game. Make all movements, player and otherwise, feel fluid but carry weight. Make it from a third person perspective. See attached image for art reference. Dont be afraid to spawn multiple agents to accomplish tasks faster.

"Power World" means **Palworld**.
